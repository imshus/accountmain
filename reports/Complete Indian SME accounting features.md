# Build the books auditors and GSTN accept

**In 2026 a "proper" Indian SME accounting app is defined less by a feature list than by three legal facts: every company's books must sit in software whose edit log cannot be switched off (for financial years from 1 April 2023), GSTR-3B's outward liability has been locked to whatever GSTR-1/1A reported since the July 2025 tax period, and TDS has run under Section 393 of the Income-tax Act 2025 since 1 April 2026.** Accounts.ai should therefore build its double-entry core in an order that writes compliance into the schema first: an append-only, audit-logged voucher store with period locks; then Tally's 28-group chart of accounts, opening balances and eight voucher types that post GST, round-off, TDS and bill-wise references as signed integer-paise lines; then day, cash and bank books, trial balance, P&L, balance sheet and ageing; then a bank reconciliation that matches on amount first and closes the bank reconciliation statement (BRS) to zero; and then GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B computed from vouchers and diffed against portal data, with GSTR-2B and Invoice Management System (IMS) reconciliation. TDS, e-invoice and e-way bill, multiple GSTINs and basic inventory come next, and cost centres, budgets, multi-currency and payroll come last, which is exactly how Zoho tiers its plans and how Tally ships those options switched off. For the jewellery client inventory jumps the queue, because AS 2 forbids LIFO, tagged diamond pieces need specific identification, every party and item needs a second ledger in fine grams (net weight × tunch ÷ 100), and 3% GST applies to the full jewellery value even when the buyer settles the metal in gold. Several inputs are still moving — GSTN's Table 4 ITC lock, the new TDS payment codes and return forms, and the GST value of "Wt"-settled wholesale sales — so lock dates, tax rates and section references must live in dated master tables, never in code.

## Three layers define "complete", and the audit trail comes first

Six leading Indian products — TallyPrime, Zoho Books, Busy, Vyapar, Marg and myBillBook — share one core: voucher entry for sales, purchase, receipt, payment, journal, contra and credit or debit notes; party ledgers carrying the GSTIN; automatic GST; bill-wise receivables and payables; bank reconciliation; day book and registers; the trial balance, P&L and balance sheet; and GST reports. Zoho's price list is the clearest public statement of where vendors draw the line between core and advanced. Its **Free plan already includes journals, bank reconciliation, GST filing and 50+ reports**; Standard adds e-invoicing, transaction period locking and IMS; Professional adds multi-currency, custom roles and approvals; Premium adds fixed assets, budgets, cash-flow forecasting and payroll; and Elite adds warehouses, batch and serial tracking ([Zoho Books India Pricing](https://www.zoho.com/in/books/pricing/)). Tally draws the same line from the other side, shipping cost centres, interest calculation and bill-by-bill tracking **set to "No" by default** in its F11 company features ([Tally Help: Company Features](https://help.tallysolutions.com/company-features-f11-tally/)). Its release history shows compliance automation migrating into the core: e-invoice IRN and QR in Release 1.1, e-way bill in 2.0, GSTR-2B reconciliation and multiple GSTINs per company in 3.0, direct GSTR-1/3B upload in 5.0, connected banking and B2B/B2C HSN summaries in 6.0, and IMS with an Edit Log Summary in 6.1 ([TallyPrime release-wise features](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/welcome-to-tally-prime/tallyprime-features/)).

The switching barrier is the accountant, not the owner. Tally's market share is reported anywhere from 51% to over 80%, accountants' familiarity with it is the main reason businesses stay, and micro businesses will pay only about **₹0–5,000 a year** ([Turia 2026](https://turia.in/articles/indian-accounting-software-industry-report-2026/)). Setup is a measured pain point: a December 2025 study of 7,835 MSMEs found **36.8%** find setting up digital tools somewhat or very challenging ([India SME Forum META Report Card 2025](https://indiasmeforum.org/digishaastra/assets/docs/Final-META-Report-Card-2025.pdf)). Accounts.ai should therefore use Tally's group names and voucher types verbatim and export Tally-compatible data so a CA can take over the books without re-keying, and its Excel-upload onboarding should land the user in a pre-seeded chart of accounts rather than an empty one. Compliance-heavy modules — e-invoice, TDS, audit reporting — are the natural paid tier, which is where Zoho puts them.

The audit trail is the one mandate that cannot be retrofitted. The software must record an audit trail of each transaction, create an edit log of each change with the date it was made, and ensure the feature cannot be disabled ([ClearTax News](https://news.cleartax.in/mandatory-to-use-accounting-software-with-audit-trail-mca/6016/amp)). Practitioner FAQs on Rule 11(g) read this strictly: **every change must be kept, not just the latest**, an error log does not suffice, and the trail must be enabled **at the database level** even where only one user can reach the database. Auditors must report whether the feature operated all year for all relevant transactions, whether it was tampered with and whether it was preserved, and they cannot rely on management's word for any of it ([TaxGuru: Rule 11(g) FAQs](https://taxguru.in/company-law/reporting-paragraphs-audit-report-faqs-audit-trail-rule-11g.html)). Secondary guidance adds that the books must be kept for **eight years** and backed up daily on servers in India under Rule 3(5), though this was paraphrased rather than checked against the gazette ([Setindiabiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/audit-trail-compliance-accounting-software-mca-guidelines)). In SQLite this translates into triggers that write before-and-after JSON into an `audit_log` table on every insert, update and cancellation of vouchers, lines, allocations and masters; a second pair of triggers that makes `audit_log` itself append-only; and a hash chain in which each log row stores the previous row's hash, so a dropped trigger or edited database file becomes detectable — which answers the auditor's tampering question directly. GST documents are cancelled rather than deleted, and every unlock of a locked period writes its own log entry. Tally's Edit Log, which records date, time and user, tracks deleted vouchers and changes to ledgers, groups and stock items, and lets users compare versions, is the reference user experience ([Tally: Audit Trail in TallyPrime](https://tallysolutions.com/tally/audit-trail-in-tallyprime/)). The obligation falls on companies; whether LLPs or proprietorships carry an equivalent duty was not established, but one schema for every entity type avoids maintaining two code paths.

The second structural fact is that **GSTR-1 now fixes GSTR-3B**. Since the July 2025 tax period the auto-populated liability cannot be edited, so errors must be fixed in GSTR-1A before 3B is filed ([A.S. Banka & Co](https://asbanka.com/2026/06/23/gstr-3b-hard-locking-gstr-1a-ims-itc/)). Table 3.2 took a turbulent path: an April 2025 advisory announced a lock from that period ([TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/advisory-on-reporting-values-in-table-3-2-of-gstr-3b.html)), GSTN kept it editable "for the time being" in May 2025 ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/gstr-3b-table-3-2-to-stay-editable-for-now-gstn)), and a December 2025 advisory locked it from the November 2025 period. **The ITC side — Table 4A locked to GSTR-2B — remains unconfirmed**: commentary expected it around July 2026, one guide says no advisory had notified it by September 2026, and one blog claims a hard block from the April 2026 return ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/big-gst-change-from-july-2026-gstr-3b-itc-locking-explained-1448389); [GSTRecoPro](https://gstrecopro.in/blogs/gstr-3b-hard-locking-2026-guide); [TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/gstr-3b-itc-hard-block-april-2026-action-plan)). Upstream, IMS sends accepted invoices into 2B, excludes rejected ones, holds pending ones, and treats no action as deemed acceptance ([Masters India](https://www.mastersindia.co/blog/invoice-management-system-ims-gst/)). The portal bars returns filed more than three years after their due date, with enforcement pushed to the November 2025 tax period ([GSTN Advisory 379](https://www.mahagst.gov.in/public/uploads/gstnadvisory/1768890686_379%20Advisory%20to%20file%20pending%20returns%20before%20expiry%20of%20three%20years.pdf); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/gstn-advisory-on-filing-pending-gst-returns-before-three-year-expiry)). Rates also changed mid-year: **from 22 September 2025 the main slabs became 5% and 18% with a 40% de-merit rate** ([GST Council press release](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/press_release_press_information_bureau.pdf)), while tobacco kept 28% plus compensation cess until 1 February 2026, when the cess ended ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fadditional-excise-duty-on-tobacco-health-cess-on-pan-masala-to-be-effective-february-1-3848089)). Every tax master — rates, HSN codes, lock dates — therefore needs valid-from and valid-to dates.

E-invoicing and the e-way bill belong in the paid tier, because many micro clients sit below the ₹5 crore threshold. No 2026 notification revising it was found, although industry analysts name a further cut as the next demand shock ([Turia 2026](https://turia.in/articles/indian-accounting-software-industry-report-2026/)). JSON export is an acceptable stopgap before direct IRP or GSP integration, but a client at or above ₹10 crore needs an "IRN not generated, N days left" alert from day one, because the IRP refuses documents reported after 30 days ([GSTN Advisory 293](https://www.mahagst.gov.in/public/uploads/gstnadvisory/1760531580_293%20Advisory%20Time%20Limit%20for%20Reporting%20e%20Invoice%20on%20the%20IRP%20Portal%20%20Lowering%20of%20Threshold%20to%20AATO%2010%20Crores%20and%20Above.pdf)).

The Income-tax Act 2025 replaced the 1961 Act on 1 April 2026. The 194-series TDS sections became rows of a table under **Section 393**, salary TDS moved to Section 392 and TCS to Section 394 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income-tax_Act,_2025)), and Forms 16, 16A and 27D became Forms 130, 131 and 133 ([TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/income-tax-act-2025-section-393-tds-new-numbers-old-194c-194j-194q)). The old Act governs a payment when the earlier of credit or payment fell on or before 31 March 2026, and quoting an old section for a new-Act transaction triggers validation errors ([TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/tds-on-contractors-w-e-f-01-4-2026.html)). TCS on sale of goods under the old 206C(1H) stopped applying from 1 April 2025, leaving only the buyer's TDS on purchases ([Income Tax India](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/tds-on-purchase-of-goods)). Two long-standing rules carry over: where GST is shown separately, TDS is computed on the value **excluding GST** ([CBDT Circular 23/2017](https://itatonline.org/info/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/CBDT-Circular-23-TDS-GST.pdf)), and a deductee without PAN suffers the higher of the specified rate and **20%** (5% for the purchase-of-goods rows) under s.397(2) ([TDSMAN](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/03/tds-tcs-rate-chart-fy-2026-27/)).

Two items were not researched and should be scoped before step 4 closes: the full Schedule III text and the MSME payables tracking that TallyPrime 7.0 added through its revised MSME Form 1 annexure.

## Eight voucher types reduce to signed paise lines and bill allocations

The engine rests on one convention. Every amount is stored as **integer paise** in STRICT tables, because SQLite's `DECIMAL(12,2)` is silently a binary float ([SQLite Datatypes](https://www.sqlite.org/datatype3.html)), and every voucher line is signed Dr-positive, Cr-negative, so "debits equal credits" becomes `SUM(amount_paise) = 0` and a trial balance becomes a single `GROUP BY ledger_id`. The chart seeds Tally's **28 predefined groups and its two predefined ledgers**, Cash and Profit & Loss A/c, the latter carrying the previous year's result as its opening balance ([Tally Help: Ledgers](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/accounts/ledgers/)). On top of that, Accounts.ai should auto-create typed ledgers with a fixed `ledger_kind`, tax head and direction, so that posting never depends on names: separate Input and Output ledgers for CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST and cess under Duties & Taxes (Tally allows one ledger for both directions, but separate ones make GSTR-3B and the set-off journal auditable), RCM-payable and RCM-input ledgers, GST on Advance Received, one TDS Payable ledger per section, TDS Receivable, and Round Off under Indirect Expenses with type "Invoice Rounding", as Tally does ([Tally Help: Round-off ledgers](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/gst-master-setup/india-gst-expense-income-non-revenue-invoice-round-off-ledgers-tally/)). Memorandum, optional and cancelled vouchers never reach the ledgers ([Tally Help: Memorandum Voucher](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs/te9rel65/Voucher_Entry/Optional_Non-Accounting_Vouchers/Memorandum_Voucher.htm)).

### Place of supply picks the tax heads, and round-off absorbs paise

Each entry below balances to the paisa and applies one rule: comparing the place of supply with the company's own state decides whether tax goes to CGST plus SGST or to IGST.

| Case | Dr | Cr | Rule applied |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intra-state sale, taxable ₹12,345.60 at 18% | Customer 14,568.00 (New Ref INV/26-27/00001) | Sales @18% 12,345.60; Output CGST 1,111.10; Output SGST 1,111.10; Round Off 0.20 | Raw total 14,567.80; Normal rounding adds 0.20, credited because it raises the receivable |
| Downward rounding, taxable ₹1,059.68 at 18% | Customer 1,250.00; Round Off 0.42 | Sales 1,059.68; Output CGST 95.37; Output SGST 95.37 | Raw 1,250.42 rounded down; round-off debited |
| Inter-state sale, ₹50,000 at 18% | Customer 59,000 | Sales 50,000; Output IGST 9,000 | Place of supply differs from company state, so IGST only |
| Intra-state purchase, ₹40,000 at 18%, ITC eligible | Purchase 40,000; Input CGST 3,600; Input SGST 3,600 | Supplier 47,200 (New Ref = supplier's invoice number) | Input ledgers are debited only on purchases |
| Blocked credit under s.17(5): staff food ₹1,000 + 18% | Staff Welfare 1,180 | Cash 1,180 | No input line; tagged "ITC ineligible" for GSTR-3B 4(D) |
| Credit note: ₹2,000 at 18% returned against INV/26-27/00001 | Sales Returns 2,000; Output CGST 180; Output SGST 180 | Customer 2,360 (Agst Ref INV/26-27/00001) | Original rate and heads; the bill's pending amount falls to 9,440 |
| Debit note: ₹5,000 at 18% returned to an inter-state supplier | Supplier 5,900 (Agst Ref) | Purchase Returns 5,000; Input IGST 900 | Reverses the ITC |

Tally offers Normal, Upward and Downward rounding to a chosen limit and shows the difference as a positive or negative figure ([Tally Help: Round off](https://help.tallysolutions.com/round-off-invoice-and-ledger-values/)). The engine should allow **one round-off line per voucher**, below ₹1 in absolute value and no more than 50 paise under Normal rounding. The Excel-register converter groups rows by invoice number, date and party, debits the party with the invoice total, credits each rate-wise Sales ledger with its summed taxable value and each output tax head with its summed tax, and sets round-off to the total minus taxable value minus tax — **rejecting the invoice when that gap reaches ₹1**, because a gap that size is a data error, not rounding. Rule 46 requires the invoice to show the taxable value after discount, tax split by head with rates, the place of supply with state name for inter-state supplies, and the reverse-charge flag ([GSTZen: Rule 46](https://gstzen.in/a/tax-invoice-cgst-rule-46.html)), so a trade discount reduces the taxable value and never posts to a Discount ledger. A mixed-rate invoice may merge its CGST into one ledger line, but it must keep rate-wise item lines because GSTR-1 and the HSN summary are reported per rate.

Notes need two extra fields. A "debit note" that some businesses send a customer for extra charges is really a supplementary invoice and posts like a sale, so GST note type and party side must be stored independently of the voucher-type name. Credit-note validation should hold cumulative notes at or below the original taxable value, match the original tax heads (an IGST invoice gets only an IGST note), and carry the original rate — including on notes against invoices dated before 22 September 2025, an inference from time-of-supply principles that still needs checking against s.14. The statutory deadline for issuing credit notes was not re-verified, so it should not be enforced until confirmed.

### TDS is credited when the expense is booked, on the value before GST

Receipts debit cash or bank and credit the party with an Agst Ref, Advance or On Account reference. When a customer has deducted TDS, the shortfall becomes a TDS Receivable that must later reconcile with Form 26AS or AIS — Dr Bank 10,800 and Dr TDS Receivable 1,000, Cr Customer 11,800 — and a settlement discount goes to Discount Allowed. Expenses subject to TDS follow a three-step pattern, shown here for a CA firm billing ₹60,000 plus 18% intra-state GST with TDS at 10% on ₹60,000.

| Step | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Book the bill | Professional Fees 60,000; Input CGST 5,400; Input SGST 5,400 | CA Firm 64,800 (New Ref = their bill); TDS Payable – 393(1) Sl. 6(iii) / old 194J(b) 6,000 |
| 2. Pay the party | CA Firm 64,800 (Agst Ref) | Bank 64,800 |
| 3. Deposit TDS by challan | TDS Payable 6,000 | Bank 6,000 |

The same shape covers a contracting firm billing ₹1,50,000 plus IGST 27,000 (Cr Contractor 1,74,000 and Cr TDS Payable 3,000, which is 2% on the pre-GST value) and office rent of ₹60,000 a month, which crosses the ₹50,000 monthly threshold and draws 10% TDS of 6,000. Each TDS ledger stores the old section, the new Section 393 reference and the payment code, and the app shows whichever the transaction date requires. The engine computes TDS as the assessable value (excluding GST) times the rate, keeps running aggregates per party per tax year so that single-payment and annual thresholds trigger correctly, applies 20% when the party has no PAN, and writes a `tds_detail` row — section, payment code, PAN, assessable value, rate, amount and date — to feed the quarterly statements. A soft warning fires when a contract, professional, rent or commission ledger crosses its threshold without a TDS line. Monthly deposit due dates under the 2025 Act were not verified, so they belong in a configurable table rather than a remembered "7th of next month".

### Reverse charge, service advances and the month-end set-off

Reverse charge takes three postings. For ₹50,000 of legal fees from an advocate at 18%, the purchase voucher is flagged RCM and posts only Dr Legal Fees, Cr Advocate 50,000; a statutory-adjustment journal then raises both the liability and the credit, debiting Input CGST–RCM 4,500 and Input SGST–RCM 4,500 and crediting RCM CGST Payable 4,500 and RCM SGST Payable 4,500; and a payment debits both payables for 9,000 against Bank. Tally uses the same sequence of reverse-charge purchase, Stat Adjustment journal and Stat Payment ([Tally Help: RCM](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs/te9rel60/Tax_India/gst/accounting_for_inward_supply_of_services_under_reverse_charge_gst.htm)). **RCM tax can be paid only in cash**, because the credit ledger pays only output tax ([TaxHeal](https://taxheal.com/pay-output-tax-cash-reverse-charge-gst-gst-itc-cant-claimed.html)), so the engine must keep RCM-payable ledgers out of every set-off against input credit.

Advances split by what is being supplied. Notification 66/2017-CT removed GST on advances for goods (except for composition dealers), but **service suppliers still owe GST when the advance arrives**, and both must issue a Rule 50 receipt voucher ([GSTZen: GST on advances](https://gstzen.in/a/gst-on-advances-received-for-future-supplies.html)). For a ₹1,18,000 advance on 18% consultancy, the advance is treated as tax-inclusive, so the tax is 1,18,000 × 18/118 = 18,000. A journal debits GST on Advance Received 18,000 and credits Output CGST and Output SGST 9,000 each, which GSTR-1 reports in Table 11A. The ₹2,00,000-plus-18% invoice then posts in full, and the advance-tax journal is reversed and reported in Table 11B. Net output tax ends at 36,000, exactly as if no advance had been taken; 18,000 of it was simply paid one period earlier.

The month-end set-off follows Rule 88A: IGST credit goes first against IGST and then against CGST and SGST in any proportion, CGST credit pays CGST and then IGST, SGST credit pays SGST and then IGST, and CGST and SGST credit never cross ([ClearTax docs](https://docs.cleartax.in/product-help-and-support/for-small-businesses/cleargst/generate-reports/itc-cash-ledger-reports/itc-ledger-reports); [TaxAdda](https://taxadda.com/order-of-utilisation-of-input-tax-credit-under-gst/)). With liabilities of CGST 20,000, SGST 20,000 and IGST 9,000 against credits of CGST 8,000, SGST 8,000 and IGST 15,000, IGST credit pays the IGST 9,000 plus 3,000 each of CGST and SGST, own credit covers a further 8,000 on each head, and 9,000 each of CGST and SGST remains for cash.

| Set-off journal | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|
| Output IGST | 9,000 | |
| Output CGST | 11,000 | |
| Output SGST | 11,000 | |
| Input IGST | | 15,000 |
| Input CGST | | 8,000 |
| Input SGST | | 8,000 |

A cash payment debiting Output CGST and Output SGST 9,000 each against Bank 18,000 then leaves all six ledgers at nil. Excess credit stays as a debit balance on the Input ledgers and carries forward; interest and late fees are expenses, never credits.

### Bill-wise settlement is a sub-ledger that must tie to the ledger

Tally defines four reference types: New Ref creates a bill on a sales or purchase entry, Agst Ref settles one on a receipt or payment, Advance records money received before the bill, and On Account records money not yet tied to any bill ([TallySolutions: Bill-wise details](https://tallysolutions.com/tally/bill-wise-details-report-in-tally-prime/)). Each party line splits into allocations of reference type, bill name, bill date, due date and amount, signed like the line itself, so an invoice's New Ref of +11,800 and a part receipt's Agst Ref of −5,000 leave a pending debit of +6,800. An Advance creates an opposite-sign bill that a later invoice adjusts by Agst Ref, while On Account money sits only in the party balance. Two invariants make the sub-ledger provable: allocations on each line sum to the line amount, and each party's ledger balance equals the sum of its bill pendings plus On Account. An Agst Ref must point at an open bill of the same party with enough pending, New Ref names are unique per party per FY, imported receipts without a reference default to On Account, and FIFO allocation against the oldest bills runs only as an explicit, labelled user action. Opening debtor and creditor balances must be split into opening bills that sum to the opening balance. Ageing runs from the bill date or the due date (bill date plus credit days); Tally's default slabs are 0–45, 45–90, 90–180 and over 180 days ([TallySolutions: pending payments](https://tallysolutions.com/tally/how-to-track-customer-pending-payments-in-tallyprime/)), while companies also need the 2021 Schedule III schedules — receivables at under 6 months, 6 months to 1 year, 1–2, 2–3 and over 3 years from due date, and payables at under 1, 1–2, 2–3 and over 3 years split between MSME and others ([KPMG India](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2021/05/chap-1-aau-schedule-III-to-the-companies-act-2013.pdf)).

### Numbering, validation, opening differences and year-end

Rule 46(b) demands a consecutive invoice serial of **at most 16 characters** using letters, digits, hyphen and slash, unique within a financial year ([GSTZen](https://gstzen.in/a/tax-invoice-cgst-rule-46.html)). Practitioners advise a fresh series every FY ([IRIS GST](https://irisgst.com/effective-1st-april-2026-reset-the-invoice-number-series/)), the limit counts the whole string including prefix and separators ([Busy](https://busy.in/gst/invoice-numbering-mistakes-under-gst-causes-corrections-and-prevention/)), and the IRP has treated invoice numbers as case-insensitive since 1 June 2025 ([IRIS GST](https://irisgst.com/?p=54609)). "INV/26-27/00001" is 15 characters, and uniqueness should be enforced on `UPPER(voucher_no)` across every series of the same document type. Purchases are keyed on supplier GSTIN, normalised supplier invoice number and FY, which also catches duplicate imports. A cancelled GST document keeps its number and is reported in GSTR-1 Table 13; it is never deleted.

Hard errors block a save: an unbalanced voucher; a date outside an open FY, before the books-beginning date or on or before the lock date; a duplicate or malformed number; a Contra containing a non-cash line, a Receipt without a debit cash or bank line, or a Payment without a credit one; CGST/SGST mixed with IGST or tax heads that contradict the place of supply; Input and Output ledgers used in the wrong direction; an invalid buyer GSTIN on a B2B sale; compensation cess on non-tobacco items from 22 September 2025 or on anything from 1 February 2026; supplier tax on an RCM voucher; a CGST-to-SGST cross set-off; and a broken bill allocation. Soft warnings let the save through but flag tax that differs from rate times value by more than ₹0.05 a line or ₹1 an invoice, negative cash, a missing TDS line above a threshold, cash or bank lines in a Journal, edits to a period whose GSTR-1 or 3B is filed (with a suggested credit or debit note instead), duplicate purchases and future-dated vouchers. Nightly invariants re-prove the books: all lines sum to zero, trial-balance debits equal credits including openings, bill pendings plus On Account equal each party balance, and item-level taxes equal the GST ledger lines.

When opening debits and credits disagree, Tally shows a "Difference in opening balances" line in the trial balance until the openings are fixed ([Tally Help: Trial Balance](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/accounting-financial-reports/trial-balance-tally/)). Accounts.ai should do the same, should not quietly park the difference in Suspense, and should block year-end close while it is non-zero. Year-end needs no closing entries in the Tally model: income and expense ledgers open the new year at zero, balance-sheet ledgers carry their closing balances forward, and the P&L A/c opening equals its prior opening plus the year's profit. Computing that on the fly means a corrected prior-year voucher flows forward automatically, and transfers to capital or reserves remain explicit journals. Closing an FY sets its status to closed, sets `lock_upto_date` to its end date and snapshots the closing balances; any later unlock is itself audit-logged, and Zoho's per-module lock dates are a good model ([Zoho Books Help: Transaction Lock](https://zstatic.zohostatic.com/books/help/accountant/transaction-lock.md)).

## Bank reconciliation matches amount first and must close to zero

The major products converge on one approach: an exact amount is the gate, a reference is the strongest tie-breaker, and date and party rank whatever remains. Tally links "Exact Matches" automatically for bulk confirmation and suggests "Potential Matches" from instrument date, amount and party within a **30-day** default window, with an option to ignore leading zeros and special characters in instrument numbers; it supports one-to-one, many-to-one and one-to-many matches, treats a manual match with disagreeing amounts as only partly reconciled, lets F7 create a missing voucher inside the match, and writes the statement date into the voucher's Bank Date when the status is set ([Tally Help: Auto Bank Reconciliation](https://help.tallysolutions.com/bank-recon/auto-bank-reconciliation/)). Zoho ranks Best and Possible Matches on date, amount and user rules and lets the user tick several book entries that sum to one bank line ([Zoho Books Help](https://www.zoho.com/us/books/help/banking/matching-transactions.html)). QuickBooks searches on amount and date, though its window is reported inconsistently — about a year back in one Intuit thread, 90 days back and 20 forward in another ([QuickBooks community](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/banking/matching-transactions-default-date-range/00/1496546); [QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/banking/bank-feed-matching/00/949100)). Xero searches on exact amount first, ranks by supplier name and reference, and breaks ties by nearest due date ([Xero Product Ideas](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/939198-for-small-businesses/suggestions/46600471-bank-reconciliation-suggestions-matching-by-in)); its automatic reconciliation draws on bank rules, existing documents, the user's past reconciliations and other users' patterns ([Xero Product Ideas: JAX](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/966939-automatic-bank-reconciliation-beta/suggestions/51046744-auto-bank-rec-improve-matching-to-existing-trans)). No vendor publishes its scoring, so the engine below is a design modelled on Tally's exact-versus-potential split.

Both sides are normalised before matching: amounts to paise; direction so that a bank credit meets a book debit to the bank ledger and vice versa; references uppercased with non-alphanumerics and leading zeros stripped; and party names uppercased with tokens such as M/S, PVT, LTD and TRADERS removed. The book side includes only bank-ledger lines with no bank date, dated on or after the reconciliation start. Each pass then sees only what earlier passes left.

| Pass | Rule | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Exact | Same direction, equal amount, equal normalised reference (cheque number, or a UTR/RRN found in the book narration), inside the date window | Auto-linked, confirmed in bulk |
| P2 Amount + party | Equal amount, party similarity ≥ 0.80, inside the window | Suggested |
| P3 Amount + date, unique | Equal amount and exactly one candidate on each side in the window | Suggested (high) |
| P4 Group | Many book lines to one bank line (deposit slip, bulk payment) or one to many (split credit, payment plus charge); bounded subset-sum of at most 6 items from 25 candidates, same party or a ±3-day cluster | Suggested |
| P5 Near-amount | Difference within a user tolerance (default ₹0), or exactly equal to a same-day charge line | Suggested with "post difference to Bank Charges" |
| P6 Classify leftovers | Narration keyword rules | "Create voucher" suggestions |

Suggestions score `50·amount_exact + 25·ref_match + 15·date_score + 10·party_score`, with `date_score = max(0, 1 − |Δdays|/window)`. A pair auto-links only at 90 or above and only when the runner-up on either side trails by at least 10 points; pairs between 60 and 90 are suggested and anything lower is hidden. Same-amount items on nearby dates — three ₹5,000 rent cheques, say — are resolved globally with `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` on cost = 100 − score rather than greedily, and several same-amount candidates in one window never auto-link without a reference match. Matches persist in a `recon_match` table with pass, score, status and user; confirming one writes the bank date onto the book line as Tally does, rejected pairs are remembered so they never resurface, and each confirmation teaches a mapping from VPA, IFSC-plus-account or narration token to the party ledger, mirroring Xero's use of past reconciliations.

Date windows should depend on the instrument. The defaults below are design choices rather than sourced banking rules and should be tuned on real statements.

| Instrument | Allowed bank date minus book date |
|---|---|
| Cheque issued | −3 to +90 days; flag as stale beyond that |
| Cheque deposited | −2 to +7 days |
| NEFT/RTGS/IMPS/UPI | −3 to +3 days (+1 for weekend batches) |
| Card, POS or gateway settlement | 0 to +5 days, net of MDR, so expect group or near-amount matches |
| Unknown | ±7 days; Tally's 30 days only on a manual "widen search" |

Leftover lines become voucher suggestions by keyword: charge markers such as CHG, CHRG, COMM, SMS ALERT, MIN BAL, AMC and IMPS or NEFT CHG suggest a bank-charges payment, merged with any adjacent same-day IGST, CGST or SGST line as input tax; INT.PD, SB INT, CREDIT INTEREST and FD INT suggest interest income; TDS suggests TDS receivable; INT DR and OD INT suggest interest expense; and REV, RVSL, RET, RTN, CHQ RET and UPI/REV trigger reversal handling. One narration-parsing guide confirms REV and RVS as refund markers and CHG and COMM as fee markers, and recommends sending ties to a review queue rather than forcing a match ([AI Accountant](https://aiaccountant.com/blog/smart-narration-parsing-indian-statements)). A debit and credit of the same amount that share a UTR or RRN, or carry a reversal keyword within seven days, are paired and matched to each other so they never reach the BRS, and a bounced customer cheque proposes reversing the receipt. Overlapping statement uploads are caught by hashing account, date, amount, direction, reference, narration and running balance.

Narrations are prefixed by payment rail and delimited by `/`, `-` or `*`, but field order differs by bank, so parsing tries per-bank templates first and generic patterns second. Reference length signals the rail — **16 characters for NEFT, 22 for RTGS and 12 digits for IMPS or UPI** — although layouts vary by bank and UPI references are not always 12 digits ([Eximpe](https://eximpe.com/blog/banking-payments/utr-number-neft-rtgs-imps-upi-payment-modes)). The generic patterns cover the RTGS UTR `[A-Z]{4}R[A-Z0-9]{17}`, the IFSC `[A-Z]{4}0[A-Z0-9]{6}`, VPAs of the form `x@y`, a 12-digit RRN accepted only alongside a UPI or IMPS marker, and six-digit cheque numbers near CHQ, CLG or CTS. SBI templates such as `TO/BY TRANSFER-UPI/DR|CR|REV/<rrn>/<name>/…` and `BY TRANSFER-NEFT*<ifsc>*<utr>*<name>`, and HDFC templates such as `UPI-<name>-<vpa>-<ifsc>-<rrn>-<remark>` and `NEFT CR-<ifsc>-<name>-…-<utr>`, were observed in sample statements; ICICI and Axis templates come from experience and must be validated against real files. Party resolution checks learned mappings first and then fuzzy-matches against party names and aliases, treating a `token_set_ratio` of 85 or more with a runner-up below 75 as resolved and anything else as a candidate list. Upload parsing must also cope with "Excel" files that are nothing of the kind: SBI's legacy ".xls" is tab-separated text and its YONO export is password-protected ([SBIXLS2Gnucash](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/abhijit86k/gnucash_statement_preprocessors/main/SBIXLS2Gnucash.py)).

The BRS starts from the book balance, adds back cheques issued but not presented and deducts bank charges not yet recorded ([ICAI BoS](https://live.icai.org/bos/vcc/pdf/Bank_Reconcilation_Statement.pdf)). Tally's screen shows the book balance, "amounts not reflected in bank", "amounts not reflected in company books", the balance as per bank, the imported statement balance and the **difference** ([Tally Help: Bank Reconciliation](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/te9rel63/Reports/Display_Registers_Ledgers/Bank_Reconciliation.htm)). With signed arithmetic, one layout serves both ordinary and overdraft accounts.

| Line | Item | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A | Balance as per books at the statement end date | Bank ledger closing |
| B1 | Add: payments and cheques issued, not yet debited by bank | Unmatched book credits dated on or before the end date |
| B2 | Add: bank credits not in books (interest, direct receipts) | Unmatched bank credits |
| C1 | Less: receipts and deposits not yet credited by bank | Unmatched book debits dated on or before the end date |
| C2 | Less: bank debits not in books (charges, NACH, OD interest) | Unmatched bank debits |
| D | Balance as per bank (computed) = A + B1 + B2 − C1 − C2 | |
| E | Closing balance of the uploaded statement | Statement |
| F | **Difference = D − E, which must be 0.00 to mark the period reconciled** | |

Before any matching, the statement must pass four integrity checks: each row's running balance follows from the previous one with zero tolerance, opening plus credits minus debits equals closing, the opening equals the previous reconciled closing, and the statement covers the BRS date. Once F reaches zero, bank dates up to that day lock and later edits to reconciled vouchers raise a warning. Accepting a "create voucher" suggestion moves the item out of B2 or C2, so the BRS must recompute live.

## GSTR-1 now fixes GSTR-3B, so compute both from books and diff the portal

GSTR-1 combines document-level tables (4, 5, 6 and 9B), which take **one row per tax rate within each invoice**, with summary tables (7, 8, 11, 12 and 13) aggregated by place of supply, rate, HSN or document series.

| Voucher condition | GSTR-1 table | Row grain |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer has GSTIN, forward charge | 4A (B2B) | invoice × rate |
| Buyer has GSTIN, recipient pays under RCM | 4B | invoice × rate |
| SEZ unit, or deemed export | 6B / 6C (in the b2b sheet) | invoice × rate |
| Export (WPAY or WOPAY, port code, shipping bill) | 6A | invoice × rate |
| No GSTIN, inter-state, **invoice value above ₹1,00,000** | 5 (B2CL) | invoice × rate |
| No GSTIN, all other cases | 7 (B2CS) | place of supply × rate × E/OE, net of B2CS notes |
| Nil-rated, exempt, non-GST | 8 | 4 fixed rows × 3 columns |
| Note to a registered buyer, or to an unregistered buyer against B2CL or export | 9B CDNR / CDNUR | note × rate |
| Advance received or adjusted (services) | 11A / 11B | place of supply × rate |
| Every outward line by HSN | 12, separate B2B and B2C tabs | HSN × UQC × rate |
| Every document series | 13 | nature × series |

Tally's export documentation follows the GSTN offline-tool sheets, sets "Rate" as the full integrated rate and limits invoice numbers to 16 characters using only "-" and "/" ([Tally Help: Exporting GSTR-1](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs/te9rel66/Tax_India/gst/exporting_gstr-1.htm)); its CDNR layout is the legacy one with the original invoice and a "Pre GST" flag, so **the current offline-tool columns must be checked before the export ships**. The B2CL threshold fell from ₹2.5 lakh to **₹1 lakh** from the August 2024 period ([Tally Help: B2C Large](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/gstr-1/b2c-large-invoices-5a-5b/)), and the test uses invoice value including tax, not taxable value. Table 12 is now split into B2B and B2C tabs, with HSN picked from a dropdown and cross-validated against the other tables, initially in warning mode ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/gstn-issued-new-advisory-on-table-12-of-gstr-1-or-gstr-1a-manual-entry-of-hsn-will-not-be-allowed/)); HSN needs four digits up to ₹5 crore AATO and six above ([Busy](https://busy.in/gst/a-guide-to-gstr-1/)); and a taxpayer with only B2C sales must still file one zero-value row in the B2B tab ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/details-mentioned-return-gstr-1)). **Table 13 has been mandatory since the May 2025 period** ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/hsn-dropdown-table-13-mandatory-in-gstr-1)), and the app should derive it from the numbering master — to minus from plus one, less cancellations — while flagging any gaps. Cancelled invoices drop out of Tables 4–7 and 12 but still count in Table 13. Because filing freezes GSTR-1, the app must keep a **filed snapshot per period** and diff later edits into amendment rows: 9A for invoices, 9C for notes and 10 for B2CS in a later period, or GSTR-1A for the same period before 3B is filed.

GSTR-3B is now largely system-populated. Tables 3.1 and 3.2 come from GSTR-1, GSTR-1A and IFF; Table 4A comes from GSTR-2B as shaped by IMS; supplier credit notes and negative 4A values land in 4B(2); and net negative GSTR-1 values appear as zero. **Table 3.1(d) omits RCM on purchases from unregistered persons and on imported services**, which the taxpayer must add by hand ([ClearTax: GSTR-3B auto-population](https://cleartax.in/s/gstr-3b-auto-population)), and reversals under Rules 42 and 43 or "for any other reasons" are the taxpayer's own determination in Table 4(B) ([CBIC Circular 170/02/2022](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/cir-170-02-2022-cgst.pdf)). The app should therefore compute every cell from its own books and diff each one against the portal.

| GSTR-3B cell | Portal source | Computation from books |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1(a) | GSTR-1 4A, 4B, 4C, 5, 6C, 7, 9, 10, 11 | Sales plus debit notes minus credit notes at rates above zero, excluding exports and SEZ, plus 11A tax minus 11B |
| 3.1(b) | 6A, 6B, 9 | Exports and SEZ supplies, with IGST where paid |
| 3.1(c) / 3.1(e) | 8 | Nil and exempt / non-GST |
| 3.1(d) | GSTR-2B, partially | All RCM purchase vouchers, tax self-computed |
| 3.2 | B2CL, inter-state B2CS, notes, advances | Inter-state subset to unregistered persons, composition dealers and UIN holders, by place of supply |
| 4(A)(1)–(5) | 2B Part A | Imports of goods (bill of entry), imports of services, domestic RCM, ISD, and other ITC **only where accepted in 2B/IMS** |
| 4(B)(1) / 4(B)(2) | 2B Part B feeds 4B(2) | s.17(5) and Rules 38/42/43 / Rule 37 unpaid-after-180-days and temporary reversals |
| 4(C), 4(D) | | Net ITC; reclaims disclosed; time-barred and wrong-state ITC |
| 6.1 | | Rule 88A utilisation, with the RCM row paid only in cash |

Two mapping questions stay open. ClearTax lists 4B (recipient-RCM) supplies among the sources of 3.1(a) even though the supplier collects no tax on them, so their exact treatment on the current portal needs confirming. And GSTN's Advisory 643 of 29 December 2025 announced **hard validation** of ITC reclaims and RCM credit against the reversal-and-reclaim and RCM statements but gave no activation date ([Busy](https://busy.in/business-updates/how-to-fix-gstr-3b-hard-block/)); until it is confirmed, the app should run the ceiling checks — 4A(5) claimed no higher than eligible tax on matched invoices, 4A(2) plus 4A(3) no higher than RCM tax paid in 3.1(d), reclaims no higher than the ledger balance — as warnings. Lock status itself should be data, a `lock_from_period` per table (3.1 from July 2025, 3.2 from November 2025, Table 4 unconfirmed), because GSTN has moved these dates several times.

The pre-filing checklist turns those rules into gates. It blocks filing when books differ from GSTR-1 per tax head (to the paisa, or by more than ₹1 after rounding), when a 3B recomputed from the GSTR-1 dataset differs from the books-based 3B, when Table 3.2 exceeds the inter-state portion of 3.1, when an inter-state B2C invoice above ₹1 lakh sits outside Table 5, when a B2B invoice above the e-invoice threshold lacks an IRN or disagrees with IRP values, or when a period falls beyond the three-year bar. It warns on drift between the filed snapshot and current books (routed to GSTR-1A, which can be filed only once per period), on Table 12 failing to tie to the other tables within ₹1, and on numbering gaps in Table 13.

On the inward side, ClearTax's GSTR-2B versus purchase-register reconciliation uses five buckets — Exact, Suggested, Mismatch, Missing in purchase register and Missing in 2B — with four actions (claim, accepting either value; keep pending; reverse; or mark ineligible), and publishes no tolerances ([ClearTax docs](https://docs.cleartax.in/product-help-and-support/for-small-businesses/gst-compliance/gstr-3b/gstr-2b-vs-purchase-register-reconciliation-a-step-by-step-guide)). Accounts.ai should key on supplier GSTIN, document type, FY and a normalised invoice number built by uppercasing, keeping only letters and digits, dropping FY tokens such as 25-26 and prefixes such as INV, BILL or TAX, and stripping leading zeros, with the digit-only string kept as a secondary key. The first pass compares date, taxable value, tax per head and tax type on the exact key, labelling full agreement Exact and anything else Mismatch with the offending fields recorded. Later passes suggest pairs under the same GSTIN whose digit strings match or whose similarity reaches 0.85 within tolerance and seven days; flag same-GSTIN pairs with equal amounts but different numbers within three days; catch same-PAN, different-GSTIN pairs as a wrong branch that the vendor must correct because ITC follows the GSTIN; and search adjacent periods, labelling hits **Timing**, because GSTR-2B does not update retrospectively and late supplier filing pushes ITC into a later period ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gstr-2b-reconciliation-mismatches-how-to-fix/)). Leftovers missing in 2B become pending ITC to chase, and those missing in the purchase register are booked or rejected or held in IMS. The default tolerance is ₹1 per tax head per invoice, configurable up to the ±₹10 that one commercial tool exposes ([ITC reconciliation tool](https://excel-utilities1.odoo.com/itc-reconciliation-tool-2-0)), and matched invoices map to IMS "Accept" while supplier-fault mismatches map to "Pending" or "Reject".

## Inventory runs on AS 2 cost layers, and jewellery adds a ledger in fine grams

AS 2 values inventory at the **lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV), item by item**, using FIFO or weighted average for interchangeable goods and **specific identification for items that are not ordinarily interchangeable** — the ICAI material names "high-value jewellery" as an example — while **LIFO is not permitted**. Cost comprises purchase price plus non-recoverable taxes, freight inward and conversion cost, less trade discounts, and excludes recoverable GST, abnormal wastage, storage, unrelated administration overheads and selling costs. Write-downs to NRV go to P&L, and raw materials are not written down if the finished goods they feed will sell at or above cost ([ICAI EIRC AS 2 Refresher, Apr 2026](https://www.eirc-icai.org/uploads/background_materials/AS%202_1777005077.pdf)). An ITAT order involving a jeweller confirms that a company is bound to FIFO or weighted average ([ITAT, Roopshree Jewellers](https://itat.gov.in/public/files/upload/1523960339-I.T.A.442&882-2015-Roopshree%20Jewellers%20P%20Ltd.pdf)), and weighted average may be computed either periodically or as each shipment arrives ([TMI extract of AS 2](https://taxmanagementindia.com/web/tmi_blog_details.asp?id=187913)). TallyPrime offers Average Cost (its default), FIFO, LIFO, Standard Cost, Last Purchase Cost and Zero Cost ([Tally Help: Stock valuation methods](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/inventory/stock-valuation-methods-tallyprime/)), but **Accounts.ai should not offer LIFO for statutory closing stock**. Income tax diverges: Section 145A and ICDS II put taxes into purchase cost whether or not credit is available ([Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://cn.lakshmisri.com/insights/articles/inventory-valuation-caught-in-the-web-of-icds-indas-and-settled-jurisprudence)), and ITAT has held the inclusive and exclusive methods tax-neutral when applied consistently across opening stock, purchases, sales and closing stock ([ITAT, Infinite Water Solutions](https://itat.gov.in/public/files/upload/1705568514-3235%20Infinite%20Water%20Solutions%20Pvt%20Ltd..pdf)). The books should follow AS 2, with a tax-audit view that restates all four figures GST-inclusive.

The three permitted formulas give materially different answers on the same data. Take fine gold at illustrative rates: an opening 100 g at ₹10,000, purchases of 200 g at ₹10,500 and 100 g at ₹11,000, and issues of 150 g and 180 g, leaving 70 g out of 400 g worth ₹42,00,000.

| Method | Cost of goods issued | Closing stock (70 g) | Write-down if year-end NRV is ₹10,200/g |
|---|---|---|---|
| FIFO | ₹34,30,000 | **₹7,70,000** (at ₹11,000) | ₹56,000 |
| Periodic weighted average (₹10,500/g) | ₹34,65,000 | **₹7,35,000** | ₹21,000 |
| Moving weighted average (₹10,333.33, then ₹10,600) | ₹34,58,000 | **₹7,42,000** | ₹28,000 |

Every method lands at ₹7,14,000 after the NRV test, and valuing gold at a year-end market rate above cost — a common trade habit — is not AS 2-compliant. The stock engine should keep **FIFO cost layers per item per godown alongside a running moving average**, compute the periodic average on demand, set the method per item and lock it once the year has transactions, and re-cost every later outward entry when a voucher is backdated, because both FIFO layers and the moving average depend on the order of entries. Tally's Stock Summary reconciles quantity but shows outwards at sales value and closing at cost, so only its profit view reconciles value ([Tally Help: Stock Summary FAQ](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/inventory-reports/stock-summary-faq-tally/)). Accounts.ai should show outwards at cost by default with sales value and gross profit as optional columns, require a godown on every stock line — Tally's own FAQ records closing value doubling when a sale was booked to a different godown from its purchase — and suppress grand-total quantities across unlike units. Closing stock is credited to the trading account and shown under Inventories on the balance sheet, becoming next year's opening stock, and any NRV write-down posts as an explicit journal that the auditor can see.

### Jewellery keeps two ledgers for every party and item

Jewellery businesses keep a rupee ledger and a **metal ledger in fine grams** side by side for every party and every stock item, where fine weight is net metal weight × tunch ÷ 100. The purity master should carry the hallmark finenesses below; of these, 9K (375) has been under mandatory hallmarking since July 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/9-carat-gold-gets-bis-hallmarking-approval-affordable-jewellery-125072100728_1.html)), while the others are the standard IS 1417 designations, whose table was not retrieved.

| Karat | 24K | 23K | 22K | 20K | 18K | 14K | 9K |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fineness | 999 / 995 | 958 | 916 | 833 | 750 | 585 | 375 |

Wholesale tunch is often quoted above hallmark purity — 18K billed at 76 rather than 75, with the excess acting as wastage or labour charged in metal. No authoritative source documents the practice, but it matches the client's register formula, so purity (karat) and tunch must be separate fields and fine weight must use the tunch stated on the voucher. Net weight should equal gross weight minus diamond carats × 0.2 minus stone weight, and the client's register must confirm whether stone weight is recorded in carats or grams. A worked line shows both settlement modes. An 18K diamond ring of 5.200 g gross carries 0.50 ct of diamonds (0.100 g) and 0.150 g of stones, giving 4.950 g net and, at tunch 76, **3.762 g fine**; diamonds are worth ₹30,000, stones ₹1,500 and labour at ₹800 a gram ₹3,960. Settled in rupees, with metal billed at an illustrative ₹12,000 per fine gram, the taxable value is ₹45,144 + 30,000 + 1,500 + 3,960 = **₹80,604**, GST at 3% is ₹2,418.12, and the invoice totals ₹83,022.12. Settled in metal ("Wt"), the party's fine ledger is debited 3.762 g and its rupee ledger only ₹35,460 plus GST. A **rate-cut** voucher later converts a chosen number of fine grams into rupees at an agreed rate; specialist Tally jewellery add-ons implement rate cuts and metal loans ([Jewels Module document](https://www.scribd.com/document/617847472/Jewels-Module)), and jewellery ERPs keep dated per-karat rates ([Ecosire ERPNext Jewellery](https://ecosire.com/es/apps/erpnext/erpnext-jewellery-management)). The key control report is the **metal position** — stock fine plus receivable fine minus payable fine — where a negative figure is unhedged exposure to the gold price.

Karigar (job-work artisan) accounts follow the same arithmetic. Issue a 100 g bar at tunch 99.50 (99.500 g fine); the karigar returns 127.500 g net of 18K jewellery at tunch 75 (95.625 g fine) and 2.000 g of scrap at tunch 92 (1.840 g fine); after an agreed 1.5% wastage allowance of 1.9125 g, the karigar still owes **0.1225 g fine**, and ₹51,000 of labour at ₹400 a gram is payable. Under AS 2, normal wastage within agreed norms becomes part of inventory cost while abnormal loss is expensed ([ICAI EIRC AS 2](https://www.eirc-icai.org/uploads/background_materials/AS%202_1777005077.pdf)), and DGFT's export wastage norms of 2.25% for handmade and 0.45% for machine-made gold jewellery offer a reference point ([BusinessWorld](https://businessworld.in/article/govt-revises-wastage-norms-for-gold-silver-platinum-jewellery-exports-537896)). Job-work law makes loss tracking a tax matter too: goods move under a Rule 55 delivery challan, the principal files ITC-04, inputs not returned within a year are deemed supplied, and because Section 143 does not cover irrecoverable process loss, unreconciled loss risks a GST demand ([ELP for GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/presentation/Gold%20jewellery%20summit-%20Nishant%20Shah.pdf)). Valuation then follows AS 2 by stock type: tagged pieces by specific identification at metal plus diamonds (at lot average) plus stones plus labour; the metal pool of bullion, karat gold and scrap at FIFO or weighted average **per fine gram**; loose diamonds at weighted average per carat within each lot of shape, sieve, colour and clarity; and certified solitaires as individual items. How to value party fine balances in rupees at year-end appears in no ICAI guidance found, so it is an accounting-policy decision for the client's CA.

### Hallmarking turns each tag into a compliance record

Under the BIS Hallmarking Order, gold jewellery sold in notified districts must carry the BIS mark, the purity in carat and fineness, and a **six-character alphanumeric HUID** ([BIS: Hallmarking – Jewellers](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-jewellers/?lang=en)), and only HUID-based hallmarks have been valid for sale since 1 April 2023 ([ClearTax News](https://news.cleartax.in/gold-with-huid-tag-to-be-sold-now-from-apr-1-23/9032/amp)). Coverage keeps widening: **380 districts from 2 March 2026** ([News On Air](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/govt-adds-7-more-districts-in-6th-phase-of-mandatory-gold-hallmarking)), 385 from 28 April 2026 according to a single secondary source ([ComplianceCalendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/mandatory-gold-hallmarking-order-2026-revised-consolidated-coverage-across-385-districts)), and another replacement annexure under the August 2026 Third Amendment whose count was not found ([TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/58507/hallmarking-of-gold-jewellery-and-gold-artefacts-third-amendment-order-2026/)). BIS exempts articles under 2 g, jewellers with annual turnover up to ₹40 lakh, kundan, polki and jadau, exports made to buyer specification, articles not substantially complete and intended for further manufacture, and bullion. Hallmarking happens once in the chain, at the first point of sale, and the invoice must show each article's description, net precious-metal weight, carat and fineness and hallmarking charges, plus a statement that the consumer can have purity verified at a BIS-recognised assaying centre; HUID on the invoice is voluntary, and the order covers gold only ([BIS](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-jewellers/?lang=en)). Because the client's diamond-studded 14K and 18K pieces fall outside the kundan and polki exemption, the software needs a unique, validated HUID field per tag together with assaying centre, date and charge; the mandatory BIS invoice lines; a district master that updates as amendments arrive; and a block on selling a non-HUID gold piece of 2 g or more unless an exemption reason is recorded. Whether the client's semi-finished B2B supplies qualify for the further-manufacture exemption should be confirmed with the client directly.

### Jewellery GST taxes the whole piece at 3%, even when paid in gold

| Supply | HSN/SAC | GST | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold, silver, platinum or diamond jewellery | 7113 | **3% on full value** (metal, diamonds, stones and making), unchanged by GST 2.0 | [Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/jewellery-hsn-code-gst-rate-classification-invoicing-guide-for-businesses/); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/learn/gst-on-diamond/) |
| Cut and polished diamonds | 7102 | 1.5% (from 18 Jul 2022) | [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/learn/gst-on-diamond/) |
| Rough diamonds | 7102 | 0.25% | [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/learn/gst-on-diamond/) |
| Job work on diamonds | 9988 | 1.5% (from Oct 2019) | [StudyCafe: 37th Council](https://studycafe.in/37th-gst-council-meeting-held-on-20th-sep-2019-recommendations-83599.html) |
| Job work on other Chapter 71 goods for a registered principal | 9988 | 5% | [Gujarat AAR 89/2020](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/AAR/guj_aar_89_2020_17.09.2020_enpte.pdf) |
| Making from a customer's own gold for an unregistered person | — | 18% (not job work) | [Gujarat AAR](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/AAR/guj_aar_89_2020_17.09.2020_enpte.pdf); [ELP for GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/presentation/Gold%20jewellery%20summit-%20Nishant%20Shah.pdf) |

This corrects a common shortcut, including one recorded in this project's earlier research: **"3% on metal plus 5% on making charges" applies only to job work for a registered principal**, and a jeweller selling its own jewellery invoices the whole value at 3% ([ELP for GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/presentation/Gold%20jewellery%20summit-%20Nishant%20Shah.pdf)). Trade blogs that tax separately shown making charges at 5% conflict with that ruling and opinion ([BankBazaar](https://bankbazaar.com/gold-rate/gst-on-gold.html)). The rate should therefore be a function of HSN or SAC, the customer's registration status and a job-work flag, not of the item alone. When old gold is exchanged, price is not the sole consideration, so **Section 15 read with Rule 27** values the supply at the market value of the new jewellery ([ELP for GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/presentation/Gold%20jewellery%20summit-%20Nishant%20Shah.pdf)). Applying the same logic to Wt-settled wholesale sales means **3% on the full ₹80,604, not on the ₹35,460 rupee portion**, with the buyer's delivery of fine gold back to the wholesaler treated as a separate taxable supply; every Wt invoice must therefore carry a metal rate from the daily rate master and print the fine weight, the rate used and the taxable value. No CBIC circular or advance ruling specific to fine-gold settlement was found, so the client's CA should sign this treatment off.

Several further rules shape the jewellery tax engine. Old gold bought from consumers is not a supply, because the individual is not acting in the course of business, and goods sent on approval move under a delivery challan and are invoiced on approval; dealers in precious metals also carry PMLA KYC duties ([ELP for GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/presentation/Gold%20jewellery%20summit-%20Nishant%20Shah.pdf)). The margin scheme for second-hand jewellery has split advance rulings — Karnataka allowed it ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/gst-leviable-only-on-difference-between-selling-and-purchase-price-of-second-hand-jewellery-aar-karnataka)), West Bengal allowed it after minor processing ([AAR](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/AAR/kundan_kumar_prasad_advance_ruling.pdf)) and a third ruling denied it ([Busy caselaw](https://busy.in/caselaw/best-money-gold-jewellery-limited-authority-for-advance-ruling-tamilnadu)). Whether inter-state jewellery movement needs an e-way bill is unresolved: one vendor guide says it does ([CodingClave](https://codingclave.com/guides/best-jewelry-shop-software-india-2026)), but jewellery may sit in the Rule 138 exempt annexure, so this needs verifying before it is built. Mainstream jewellery editions cover the basics — Busy lists purity, gross and net weight, diamonds by carat, making and stone charges, melting loss, job work, old-gold exchange, barcodes and HUID entry but no metal-balance account ([Busy: Jewellery](https://busy.in/accounting-software/jewellery-shop/)), and Tally lists weight and purity tracking, metal-loss variation and karigar accounting ([Tally: Jewellery](https://tallysolutions.com/accounting-software/jewellery/)) — while **party-wise fine-gold ledgers with rate cut and metal loans appear mainly in add-ons**, which is where Accounts.ai can differentiate.

## Conclusion

The 2026 rules change what "editing the books" means. The audit trail turns every change into permanent evidence, and the GSTR-1-to-3B lock turns every post-filing edit into a compliance event, so the app's central workflow is no longer free editing but **amendment routing** — snapshots, locks, credit and debit notes and GSTR-1A. That is why compliance belongs in the schema, and why the AI chat should sit on top of engine outputs that prove their own invariants rather than compute anything itself. The model earns its place where its judgement is cheap to verify: proposing the party behind a bank narration, triaging GSTR-2B mismatches, suggesting a ledger group or HSN code, and explaining why a BRS will not close — each suggestion then accepted or rejected by deterministic checks and by the user.

The strongest commercial opening is the jewellery vertical. Mainstream packages track weight and purity but leave the fine-gold party ledger, rate cut and metal position to add-ons, even though those are the numbers a jewellery wholesaler actually runs on. The remaining risks are mostly regulatory timing and interpretation rather than engineering: before the corresponding code paths are hard-wired, the client's CA should rule on GST valuation of Wt settlement and year-end valuation of fine balances, and the team should confirm the Table 4 ITC lock, the new TDS payment codes and statement forms, the current CDNR export columns and the e-way bill status of jewellery. Real registers from the client will settle the net-weight formula and tunch conventions faster than any further desk research.
